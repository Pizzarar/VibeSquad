# ROLO

Stop asking 'how would I build this?' Start asking 'how would I guide a team of extremely talented toddlers to build it?' They know more than you about how. You know what, why, and when it's good. Let them teach you, and you steer.

ROLO is four parts: Riff, Organize, Loop, Own.

## Riff

Talk to a [Buddy](Concepts/Buddy.md). Say what you want in your own words. Then ask it to explain, in plain English, how programs like yours work. Ask what already does this, and how. You learn the how from it. You do not need to know it first.

## Organize

Organizing starts while you talk. The [Buddy](Concepts/Buddy.md) breaks the idea down with you. Then a [Lead](Concepts/Lead.md) turns the plain plan into small jobs for workers. Each job goes to the cheapest model that can do it. The Lead checks their proof.

## Loop

Look at how bad it is. Refine. Tell the agent what you see. Do it again. Repeat until it looks or works right. You find bugs by describing what is on your screen.

## Own

You decide yes or no. The agents can build and check. Only you can say it is what you wanted.

## How it maps to the work

ROLO follows three steps: you and a Buddy, then a Lead with workers, then back to you.

- Step 1 is Riff. Start here: [You and a Buddy](Guide/01-you-and-a-buddy.md).
- Step 2 is Organize.
- Step 3 is Own.
- Loop runs through every step.

See [Start](Maps/Start.md) for all the steps in order.

> **Aub:** I kept asking the agents to build features, and each part they made was perfect, but together it didn't work. Neither of us was thinking about how the parts fit into the whole. We were only thinking about how to build *that one thing*. Then it clicked. They already know how to build each part. My job is to figure out what the parts are, then guide them on how the parts connect.
